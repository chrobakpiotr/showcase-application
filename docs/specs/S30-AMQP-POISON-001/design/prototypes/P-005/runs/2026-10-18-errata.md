# P-005 evidence errata — 2026-10-18

This errata corrects interpretation of the immutable 2026-10-17 artifacts. The
original `probe.py`, `report.json`, and `run-metadata.json` are retained
byte-for-byte; their SHA-256 values remain those recorded in the README and
metadata. This note is not a rerun or a qualification.

## Observations

- In the original probe, both management DELETE calls use `session`, whose
  authenticated principal is `p005admin` (`ADMIN`). They are administrator
  actions. The `p005fencer` identity was only checked for `GET /api/users` and
  quarantine queue retrieval. No connection deletion was performed by, or
  attributed to, the fencer. Therefore the observed HTTP 204/404 results say
  nothing about fencer close permissions.
- The held-handler scenario observed a stale synthetic effect refusal, failed
  ACK, and redelivery after connection closure. Those remain broker/handler
  observations with their stated fixture boundaries.
- The timeout injection observed a client-side `requests.Timeout` from a local
  response-holding TCP sink.
- The successful reconnect sample had no channel or consumer on either
  connection. The original `/api/connections` recheck is not evidence that no
  consumer remained; in another observation it disagreed with
  `/api/channels` and `/api/consumers`.

## Fixture assumptions

- The workload-to-Rabbit-user mapping, PostgreSQL generation row and effect
  table were synthetic fixture state. The generation check was a sequential
  row-lock/read followed by an insert; the probe did not exercise a conditional
  write/CAS concurrent with a fence transition.
- The existing close path re-read the observed connection record before
  DELETE, but a read-then-delete sequence still has a race between those
  operations. Revalidation narrows stale-target risk; it does not remove the
  TOCTOU window.
- The existing management DELETE was made by the administrator fixture
  principal. No operation-scoped least-privilege authority was tested.

## Properties not tested by the 2026-10-17 run

- Admission response to the outcome of a fencing request. `admission_open` was
  assigned `False` before the request and was never changed based on its
  result. The timeout assertion was tautological. The run proves transport
  timeout injection only; it proves no fail-closed admission behavior.
- Absence of channels/consumers after close, a settled drain, or agreement of
  `/api/connections`, `/api/channels`, and `/api/consumers` after close.
- Any channel/consumer identity or usability after reconnect.
- A same-transaction conditional effect write racing a fence.

## Checkable admission response for a future integrated design

Keep the admission state `INHIBITED` while the fence result is `UNKNOWN`,
`TIMEOUT`, `FAILED`, or lacks complete broker/drain proof; in those outcomes do
not issue a drain acknowledgement. A confirmed close alone may advance the
episode to `FENCED_PENDING_DRAIN`, never to `ACTIVE`. Only verified settled
drain plus the existing durable, audited RESUME transition may release
admission. Tests must feed actual typed outcomes and assert the resulting
state/acknowledgement; no constant-state assertion is evidence. This is a
proposed state transition for the integrated contract, not code or behavior
tested by P-005.

## Docker anonymous-volume correction

The pinned RabbitMQ image declares `VOLUME /var/lib/rabbitmq`. The earlier statements “no persistent volumes” and “no volume” meant no explicit host bind or named mount; they did not account for Docker's image-declared anonymous volume. The original P-005 `docker rm --force` commands omitted `--volumes`, so removal of the containers did not remove their anonymous volumes. The P-005 primary run's exact per-container command/volume mapping was not retained. The two follow-up volumes were later matched by creation time to their recorded broker-start times and reconciled; see the P-006 volume reconciliation report. Other volumes potentially associated with P-005's exploratory runs remain unassigned and were not removed.

The corrected P-005 runner now records `docker volume ls` before and after each run, inspects the broker's mount list, and removes containers with `docker rm --force --volumes`. A cleanup failure or a still-present recorded Rabbit volume makes the run `CLEANUP_FAILED`.
